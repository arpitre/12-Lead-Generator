What you can do with it

Build the case you're teaching

Pick a rhythm, then stack findings the way real patients present them — an inferior STEMI with a Wenckebach with muscle tremor artifact. Rate, ectopy, AV block, axis, bundle branch and fascicular block, pre-excitation, pacing, infarct territory and stage, chamber enlargement, electrolytes, overdose patterns, right-sided and posterior leads.

Test without giving it away

Blind mode hides the measurements, the machine interpretation, the case name and the whole control panel. The student gets a tracing and nothing else — the way it arrives on a real call. Share the link and it opens blind on their device too.

Show the why, not just the answer

Reveal walks the tracing in the order it should be read: rate, rhythm, axis, intervals, morphology, ST segments, then the anatomy — which wall, which artery, and what complication to expect next. It's the explanation a good preceptor gives over a coffee, attached to every case.

Measure it like paper

Drag the on-screen calipers across any lead for real time and voltage readings. The tracing is drawn at a true 25 mm/s and 10 mm/mV on real ECG paper, so a box is a box — and it prints that way for a written exam.

  3. 3

    The walkthrough gives the reasoning step by step, so the debrief is about the electricity rather than about who guessed right.

Why the tracings hold up

Most practice ECGs are drawings. Someone sketched an ST elevation into three leads and left the rest of the tracing alone — so a student who looks for the reciprocal change finds nothing, and quietly learns that it doesn't matter.

This one simulates the heart as an electrical vector in three dimensions and projects that vector onto all twelve leads. Move the axis and every lead responds. Infarct a wall and the leads facing it elevate while the leads opposite it depress, because that is what the geometry does.

The result is a tracing that rewards the habits you want students to build: comparing III to II, checking aVL before believing an inferior STEMI, walking the precordium for R-wave progression.

It also means the case library isn't a fixed set of pictures. Any combination of findings you can describe clinically, the model can render — including the ugly ones with artifact on top.
